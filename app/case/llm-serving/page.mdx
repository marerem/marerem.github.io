## The idea

Often you don't need an LLM to *write* an answer. You need it to *decide*: yes or no, a score, or one option from a list. A Jev-style readout gets that decision from **one forward pass**, without generating any text.

## How the readout works

1. The prompt holds the state (any free-form context), the instructions, and the allowed answers.
2. The model runs **one forward pass**.
3. From the final logits, I keep only the tokens of the **allowed answers**, for example ` yes` and ` no`.
4. A **softmax over just those tokens** gives the probabilities.

With two candidates, this is simply a sigmoid of the logit difference, so the output is a calibrated probability rather than a sampled word. It's not top-k sampling: the answer can only ever be one of the allowed options.

## Three readouts

- **Noul:** a yes/no decision, as a probability
- **Score:** a value on a scale
- **Choice:** one option from a fixed list

## Serving it

I self-host the model with **vLLM**, whose **PagedAttention** manages the KV cache efficiently, and run it on **Kubernetes** in Docker containers.

## What I learned

- **Constrain the output, don't parse it.** Reading the logits of allowed tokens is more reliable than asking the model to answer and parsing free text.
- **One forward pass is cheap.** Skipping generation makes each decision fast, which matters when you need many of them.
