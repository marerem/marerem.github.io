# Jev-style decisions from an untuned LLM

*Draft — the numbers in the diagrams are illustrative, not measured.*

A decision model like Jev doesn't generate text. It answers a **typed question** — *noul* (yes/no), *score* (ordered levels) or *choice* (K options) — with a probability distribution over the allowed answers. You can get that from an off-the-shelf LLM without training: one forward pass, keep the logits of the allowed answer tokens, softmax over them.

## Only the candidate list changes

The mask is **fixed by the question type**, not by what the model happens to rank highest. For noul it is always `" yes"` and `" no"` — even when `" Yes"` scores higher than `" no"`. Switch the type and turn the mask on:

<CandidateMask />

Before the mask, the candidates' raw probabilities don't sum to 1 — the rest of the mass sits on every other token. After it, we have a proper conditional distribution:

$$
P(a \mid \text{prompt},\ a \in \mathcal{A}) = \frac{e^{\ell_a / T}}{\sum_{a' \in \mathcal{A}} e^{\ell_{a'} / T}}
$$

## With two candidates, softmax is a sigmoid

For noul, $\mathcal{A} = \{\text{yes}, \text{no}\}$ and the softmax collapses to a function of a single number, the logit gap $\Delta = \ell_{\text{yes}} - \ell_{\text{no}}$:

$$
P(\text{yes}) = \sigma\!\left(\frac{\Delta + b}{T}\right)
$$

Calibration fits $T$ and the yes-bias $b$ on the validation split. Drag the point along the curve, then move the sliders — $T$ stretches the curve (less confident), $b$ shifts it:
